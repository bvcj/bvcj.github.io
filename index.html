01 — The question

Technology is turning toward the psyche.

Ukraine can lead the new field of recovery technologies.

01 / CONTEXT Why Overand

An entire society living through change.

Entering its fifth year of full-scale war, Ukraine's army includes hundreds of thousands of civilians who, only a few years ago, were designers, artists, lawyers, engineers, or office workers. As they cycle in and out of military rotations, society as a whole is undergoing profound change.

Ukraine has become a global center for defense innovation, yet only a small share of that innovation focuses on how people survive and recover from hyper-mediated warfare. Mental health and social recovery tools remain generic. We see this asymmetry as a critical tech-justice problem.

02 — An emerging field

Technology is turning
toward the psyche.

Ukraine can lead the new field of recovery technologies.

The technology industry everywhere is turning toward the psyche: attention, sleep, stress and mood are now design problems, increasingly addressed through biometric wearables, spatial and binaural audio, VR exposure environments, neurofeedback, and conversational AI positioned as a first line of psychological support.

Nearly all of this is calibrated for peacetime cognition, with little research on how contemporary war affects the mind. Decades after Harun Farocki documented video games being used to treat PTSD, post-war recovery demands a new body of work.

Ukraine can become a leader in this emerging field, creating open-source knowledge that can be adapted elsewhere.

04 / FIELDWORK Kyiv, Ukraine

The Kyiv music programme
+ psychotherapy

Sound is not only something we experience. It can become something we shape, understand, and use to recover a sense of agency.

01 — The problem

Sonic warfare

Psychiatrists at Kyiv's military hospital use “dronophobia” for a new subset of shell shock: scooters, fans, air conditioners, and bees can reproduce the sound of a drone and trigger panic in civilian life.

02 — The method

Sound as recovery technology

Across 32 music-composition lessons interlaced with collective psychotherapy, workshops, and listening labs, participants transform feared sounds into material they can shape, compose, and perform.

The shift is from being involuntarily affected by sound to becoming its author — gaining agency over material that resists ordinary speech.

03 — The gathering

A protected graduation rave

The programme culminates in a protected graduation rave, where soldiers share creative work and wartime experiences with civilians.

06 / A WORKING DEFINITION

What are recovery
technologies?

01

A method, tool or protocol for cognitive health, collective agency and a return to public life — not only a clinical treatment.

02

Built with those who use it: veterans, soldiers, civilians, clinicians, engineers and artists in one room.

03

Tested in real settings, not laboratories — studios, clubs, clinics and homes.

04

Documented and open-source, so knowledge produced in Ukraine can travel, change and be used elsewhere.

Sound is the first field of practice. Further research extends this model into spatial audio, AR/VR, biosensors, neurofeedback, and AI-assisted therapeutic tools grounded in clinical and lived reality.

The Overand Institute for Recovery Technologies is a nonprofit in formation, founded by professor Bogna Konior and psychotherapist Yuliia Leites.

Working at the intersection of emerging technologies and psychotherapy, we study how technologically accelerated modern warfare affects the human psyche. We develop open-source tools and homecoming protocols that support cognitive health and collective agency.
